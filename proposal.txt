Proposal 1 (preferred): Digital Payments (UPI) and Financial Inclusion across Indian States This project asks whether states that adopted digital payments faster also became more financially inclusive and grew faster, and in which direction the effect runs. Background India has built one of the world's most visible digital payment systems around UPI, launched in 2016 and operated by NPCI, alongside the Jan Dhan account drive that began in 2014. Policy discussion assumes that digital payments widen access to formal finance and support growth, but the evidence at state level is thin. Your own work on financial inclusion, ICT development and growth across Indian states shows that these three move together; digital payments are the natural next layer, because they sit exactly where financial and ICT platforms converge. Problem statement Digital payment adoption differs sharply across Indian states, driven by internet access, bank branch density, income and literacy. It is unclear whether higher adoption leads to deeper financial inclusion, or whether states that were already more inclusive simply adopted faster. Without separating these, policy cannot tell where a digital payment push will actually help. Objectives 1 To measure how digital payment adoption has grown across Indian states over the available period. 2 To test the relationship between digital payment adoption and financial inclusion. 3 To test the relationship between digital payment adoption and state-level economic growth. 4 To identify the direction of causality (adoption to inclusion, inclusion to adoption, or both). 5 To draw policy implications for lagging states. Research questions and hypotheses • RQ1: Does higher digital payment adoption raise financial inclusion in a state? (H1: yes, positively.) • RQ2: Does adoption contribute to state-level economic growth after controlling for other drivers? (H2: yes, positively.) • RQ3: Is the causality bidirectional? (H3: yes; inclusion also encourages adoption.) • RQ4: Does the effect differ between better-connected and weaker-infrastructure states? (H4: stronger where internet access is higher.) Methodology I will build a panel of Indian states over the available years and proceed in stages: descriptive analysis and trends; panel unit root tests to check stationarity; fixed-effects and random-effects regressions (with a Hausman test to choose between them); and Granger-type causality tests to examine direction. If time permits, I will extend to a panel VAR with impulse responses, which is a method used in your published work. Software: Stata, R or Python. Expected outcomes • A state-wise picture of digital payment adoption and financial inclusion. • Evidence on whether adoption supports inclusion and growth, and in which direction. • A short list of policy suggestions for states that lag, such as investing in connectivity and agent networks.
